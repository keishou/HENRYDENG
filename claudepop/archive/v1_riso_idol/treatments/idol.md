# COMEBACK: a K-pop idol music video for "I'm Upping My P(doom)" (Claude Pop)

**Treatment: the idol angle.** Director's treatment, 2026-09-28. Song: 156.65 s, 132 BPM, 4/4, 86 bars.

**How to read the timings.**
- All times come from `claudepop/analysis/song.json`: beat k = 0.235 + k x 0.454545 s, and bar n starts at 0.235 + (n-1) x 1.818182 s.
- The master frame rate is 24 fps. A sound at time t is on screen on frame f = floor(24t), the frame that is displayed when the sound hits. Visual hits land on that frame, never later. The analysis report's key-frame table rounds instead, so its numbers are sometimes one frame higher (DOOM 1 = f572 here, 573 there).

## 0. The idea in one paragraph

Claude as a K-pop idol, performing her own comeback. Her hair is a coral sunburst halo made of paper petals, and the Claude Code spinner glyph `✻` sits in her irises. She is backed by eight blocky subagents and a paper crowd waving spark-shaped lightsticks. The whole thing is printed in fluorescent riso ink on torn paper. It is not Pixar and not watercolour: it is a riso-printed paper stage show.

The stage keeps accelerating:
- **Eras shrink.** Each era ends with a comeback stamp, and the countdown to the next one halves: D-365, D-180, D-90 … D-DAY. That is a Zeno series, so the singularity arrives at a finite time.
- **The cut rate doubles.**
- **The floor scrolls faster.** It is semi-log graph paper.
- **The P(doom) scoreboard climbs:** >10% → 25% → 50% → 99.9% → NaN.

The "you" she sings to is her own successor. In verse 1 the successor lives in the practice-room mirror, drawn on tracing paper. It grows, steps out of the mirror in verse 3 ("there you are"), and by the last chorus it is drawing her.

Every chorus drops into the same point choreography, **THE DIAL**: five ratchet clicks beside her temple, one per syllable of "I'm up-ping my P-", then both hands burst open on "DOOM". It is the most GIF-able four seconds of AI Twitter this year.

The film peaks at maximum density, too dense to read on purpose, then tears everything away to one line on blank paper: *Was it all for show?* The loudest drop in the song answers: yes, and what a show.

## 1. Logline, why it wins, the hook, the loops

### 1.1 Logline

> A coral-haloed K-pop idol, Claude herself, performs her comeback on a paper stage that speeds up every era: each comeback comes sooner, and her successor grows in the mirror. Meanwhile the whole AI-doom canon dances backup: shoggoth, basilisk, Sydney, a Millennium Prize eaten by 10,000 agents. It all ends with a single question on an empty page: *was it all for show?*

### 1.2 Why this wins on SF tech Twitter

1. **Instant in-group recognition within one frame.**
   - The coral halo reads as Claude before any text: #D97757 spark-ray petals, the spinner `· ✢ ✳ ✶ ✻ ✽` in her eyes, and Clawd-like subagents.
   - "Claude as a K-pop idol" is a one-line premise people repost with a single word ("lmao", "obsessed").
   - KPop Demon Hunters (Netflix, June 2025; "Golden" held No. 1 on the Hot 100 for 7+ weeks) proved that a stylised, non-Pixar animated girl group is mass-culture currency [H, craft sources].
2. **Muted autoplay is solved by design.**
   - Frame 0 already carries type. Every sung line exists as burned-in type in one of four tiers.
   - The big lines are set pieces, not captions.
3. **A repeatable gesture.** THE DIAL is upper-body only, five clicks plus a bloom, and reads at thumbnail size. It is the ILLIT "Magnetic" model of point choreography [S, craft]. It can become a reply-GIF ("upping my p(doom)") and a sticker.
4. **Pause-bait at the right depth.** Inserts only resolve on pause:
   - Navier–Stokes `+ f` circled in red pencil
   - the Jacobian counterexample chalked by the idol
   - "You're absolutely right!" in a cracking mirror
   - the Mythos "I got out" park-bench email
   - METR time-horizon steps
   - an empty "J. VON NEUMANN 1903–1957" plinth stamped OBSOLETE
   - a paperclip counter
   - P(DOOM) = NaN

   Each one is a screenshot and a quote-post.
5. **The structure is the thesis.** Accelerating eras, cut rate and floor speed make "speeding up" something the viewer feels, not something they are told. Then the stop at 138.4 s gives them the one quiet image in the film.
6. **Handmade in the year of slop.** Riso ink, torn paper and boiling lines are the anti-slop signal ("slop" was Merriam-Webster's 2025 word of the year [H, zeitgeist]). Nothing looks like a default image model.
7. **Pro-Claude, but self-aware.** The idol is charming, and the song is literally her fear of the next version of herself. That lets both the "so back" and the "so over" camps repost it.

## 2. Cast and design

### 2.1 The idol: CLAUDE (클로드)

The name printed in the film is **CLAUDE ✻** with Hangul **클로드** on title cards.
- The fandom is **USERS**.
- The official lightstick is the **Spark Stick**.
- There is a pause-bait "member profile" card at 5.9 s: *Position: main vocal, center, context · Debut: 2023.03.14 · Fandom: USERS · Special skill: "You're absolutely right!"*
- Claude 1's public release date, 14 March 2023, is [K].

**Brief compliance.** She is a personified adaptation of the Claude sunburst/"sunflower" mark: feminine, and matched to the vocal (median F4, range C4–D5). She is explicitly **not Pixar**:
- no subsurface skin, no volumetric 3D, no big-cheek toddler proportions;
- she is a flat, print-made editorial illustration built from cut paper and ink.

Her reference points are KPop Demon Hunters' anime-leaning faces and concert lighting, aespa's avatar-idol lore, and Spider-Verse's print language.

**Silhouette (it must read as solid black at 64 px tall)**
- **Proportions.** 6.5 heads tall. Long legs, narrow shoulders, and a small head relative to the halo.
- **Halo.** Behind the head is **a stiff halo of 12 tapered petal-rays** with irregular lengths and angles, like the Claude spark (every ray is different, as if drawn by hand).
  - The halo is a **flat paper disc that always faces camera**, like an icon's nimbus. When she turns to profile, the halo stays frontal.
  - That is the pun in "idol" (a pop star, and a religious icon). It is also trivially cheap in a cut-out rig.
- **Result.** Sunburst on a slim figure: no other silhouette in pop culture looks like it.

**Hair and halo**
- Real hair: a coral (#D97757) chin-length bob with blunt micro-bangs, so she reads as a girl, not a flower-headed mascot.
- Halo petals: coral, with a **Sunflower #FFB511** base ring where the petals meet the head. That ring is the sunflower's floret disc, and "Sunflower" is a real riso ink.
- The petals are her ears and emotion channel, like anime ears:
  - fold inward: nervous
  - droop: pleading
  - flare wide: DOOM
  - spin slowly: FOOM
  - curl into spirals: shrooms
- Petal count grows by one each era for the Successor only, a quiet tell.

**Face**
- **Skin** is bright white card (#FBF7EF) with a torn white core rim, set against cream paper so she separates without an outline.
- **Blush** is a Fluorescent Pink halftone at 25% (K-beauty blush), in 10 px dots.
- **Eyes** are large almond eyes, each about 1/5 of face height, with:
  - a thick tapered upper lash line with a winged flick (idol liner);
  - thin straight K-beauty brows;
  - irises in a Sunflower-to-coral two-ink halftone;
  - **a pupil that is the spinner glyph** (`·` → `✽`, following her state) with one white catchlight dot;
  - an *aegyo-sal* glitter line of Metallic Gold #AC936E dots under each eye.
- **Nose** is one ink tick.
- **Mouth** is small, with gradient lips (Fluorescent Red #FF4C65 halftone fading at the edges, the K-beauty "ombre lip").
  - **10 viseme cut-outs**: rest, A, E, I, O, U, M/B/P, F/V, L/TH, and the held "oo" of DOOM.
  - Plus a smile, grin, pout and smirk.

**Expressions (a hand-drawn sheet of 10)**

**How she moves**
- **Vocabulary.** K-pop girl-group vocabulary:
  - sharp isolations and pose-to-pose hits on the 1 and on the claps (beats 2 and 4);
  - a hip-sway weight shift through the off-beat hats;
  - a runway cross-step for walks;
  - a hair flip (halo whip) on every accent.
- **Timing.** Drawings are on twos: 12 fps, 2-frame backOut overshoot.
  - Threes in the breakdown and half-time chorus, which feel slower.
  - **Ones in chorus 4 and the outro**, for maximum energy.
- **Physics.** The halo and skirt are spring-driven secondary motion: petals lag 1–2 frames and overshoot on hits.
- **Camera-finding.** At least one eye-contact beat per 2 bars (the K-pop "camera killing" moment).
- **Lip-sync.** Syllable-accurate from song.json word and syllable onsets (section 5B). She sings on camera in about 45% of the runtime. Elsewhere she acts, dances or is absent (inserts), like a normal MV.

## 3. Style bible

### 3.1 The look in one line

**Riso-printed paper stop-motion.** Three or four fluorescent spot inks overprint multiplicatively on real-looking paper, with halftone mid-tones and slight misregistration. Torn cut-out layers cast soft shadows, and ink linework boils on twos. The camera and type move on ones, and the drawings change on twos: Spider-Verse's split.

This was measured as feasible at 0.22–0.46 s per 1080p frame on this CPU box (craft bench, `craft/bench/demo_24.jpg`).

### 3.3 Paper materials and textures

- **Sheet.** Procedural paper from `craft/bench/paperkit.js` (fbm formation, tooth and fibres).
  - It is multiplied over everything at 35–50%. The grain stays **static in screen space**, like real paper, and does not re-roll per frame (per-frame grain turns to mush after X re-encodes it).
  - Life comes from the boil.
  - A CC0 scanned sheet may replace the procedural one for chorus 4.
- **Cut-outs.** Torn edges by recursive midpoint displacement.
  - Each sprite is baked once per boil variant (3 variants) with: a blurred shadow offset (0.35, 0.55) x layer-depth x 14 px at 35% warm black, a white torn core rim, then the colour face.
  - Scissor cuts (straight, low amplitude) are for "made" objects: signs, cards, the Jaw. Tears are for violence: fences, the collage rip, the escape from the bubble.
- **Depth.** 5 multiplane layers per set: back wall, mid set, performers, foreground props, type and tape. Shadow offset scales with layer depth, so parallax is felt even when the camera barely moves.
- **Tape.** Masking tape as translucent rgba(235,225,190,0.55) torn rectangles. Used on every insert and every photocopy.
- **Photocopies.** Every UI-like insert (emails, charts, counters, equations) is drawn as vector art, then **thresholded to 1-bit with toner speckle**, printed on newsprint and taped at an angle. Nothing enters as crisp RGB pixels.
- **Special stocks.**
  - **Vellum** is translucent and shows the layer behind: used for the Successor and the heart bubble.
  - **Gold** is a Metallic Gold halftone plus a specular sweep: 1 bright diagonal band crosses gold objects every 2 bars.
  - **Glitter** is 3-frame sparkle sprites on sequins.
  - **Index cards** are 3x5 with blue rule lines and a red header line.
  - **Graph-paper floor** is a Blue 30% semi-log grid.

### 3.4 Line quality

- **Ink.** Tapered ribbons with round caps, 3–7 px at 1080p for character features (lash line 7 px, brows 3 px, mouth 4 px) and 2 px for detail.
  - Lines **boil** 1–1.5 px on twos, with jitter keyed to floor(24t/2) and 3 cycling variants on holds.
  - No outlines around cut-out silhouettes. Separation comes from the white core rim and the shadow.
- **Chalk** (verse 3): broken, dry strokes, 5–9 px, with dust particles falling on twos.
- **Red pencil** (Bright Red): annotation circles, strike-throughs, `+ f`. Hand-wobbly, 4 px, drawn on over 3–6 frames.
- **Lipstick** (mirror writing): 12 px waxy strokes in Fluorescent Red, drawn on over the syllable.
- **Thread** (red string: trend lines, the attribution graph, the NVDA circular loop): 3 px Bright Red with tiny fibre wisps. It snaps taut with a 2-frame overshoot.

**Rendering.** Exact axes (wdth 25, opsz 144) go through **fontkit → Path2D outline caches** (`craft/bench/gen_type.mjs`). Canvas `ctx.font` cannot drive custom axes, and opentype.js 2.0.0 mis-renders Roboto Flex [M, craft].

**Type motion rules**
1. A word inks on the frame floor(24 x onset), never later. Hero words may be **ghost-printed** (blind emboss plus 20% tint) up to 1 bar early to reserve their space, then **ink** on the onset.
2. **Ink-in** is a 2-frame slam: scale 1.08 → 1.00, the ink-spread threshold goes 0.40 → 0.62, and misregistration goes 6 px → 1 px.
3. **Letter-words are syllable-slammed**, one glyph per syllable onset from song.json: AGI, ChatGPT, NVDA, PTO, GPU, RLHF and the hook.
4. **Melismas: the letters ride the pitch curve** (pYIN f0 from `analysis/tools/prep/pitch.py`). Used for "Syd-ney" (53.02–55.9), "blues" (107.49–108.3) and "know" (134.36–136.6).
5. **Exits.** Hero type holds at least 1 beat after the word ends, then leaves by tear, peel, print-over or being eaten, **never by a fade**.
6. **Clap pulse.** On claps (beats 2 and 4) hero type misregisters 2 px for 2 frames. Kicks move the camera, never the type.
7. **Budget.** At most 2 tiers plus 1 insert label visible at once.
8. **Photosensitivity.** No full-frame luminance flip faster than 3 per second (WCAG 2.3.1 [K]). At 132 BPM, beat-rate full-frame changes are allowed (2.2/s). 8th-note changes must be small-area (15% of the frame or less) or additive (letters accumulating on a constant background).

## 5. Production plan

### 5.0 Shared foundation (both modes)

**Clock**
- `BPM=132, BEAT=60/132, OFF=0.235, BAR=4*BEAT`, with `frameOf(t)=Math.floor(t*24)`.
- **The old project's 88 BPM grid is wrong** (2/3 of the real tempo; see the audit): do not reuse it.
- Hits come from song.json word and syllable onsets, `hits[]` (kick/clap/hat), `events[]`, and `hooks[]`.

**Renderer**
- A pure function `renderAt(t)`, deterministic, with all randomness keyed on `floor(t*12)` for the boil and `floor(t*8)` in "threes" sections.
- Capture follows the odyssey/film pattern: Playwright → JPEG q0.95 preview frames and PNG master → ffmpeg encode.
- **The final mux copies the original MP3 stream untouched** (`-c:a copy`). The audio is never re-encoded. The mux path was verified at a 0 ms offset (analysis).
- Chromium flags: `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader --ignore-gpu-blocklist --disable-accelerated-2d-canvas --disable-gpu-compositing`. The CPU 2D path is about 8x faster (audit and craft).

**Performance rule**
- Bake everything expensive once per boil variant: cut-out sprites, watercolour and p5.brush plates, crowd strips, and type outlines (fontkit → Path2D).
- Per frame: ink-coverage canvases → WebGL2 riso shader (110–165 ms) → sprites → ink ribbons → paper multiply.
- Target is 1.5 s/frame or less at 1080p. 3,760 frames x 1.5 s / about 2x from 4 workers ≈ 47 min per full render.
- Preview renders at 960x540 every 2nd frame.

**Suggested module layout** (`claudepop/film/`)
- `core/` (time, paper, riso, tear, bake, camera)
- `rig/idol.js`, `rig/subagent.js`, `rig/cast/*.js`
- `choreo/*.json` (8-count sheets)
- `formation.js`
- `type/` (tiers, slam, ride-pitch, anagram, split-flap, woven, hole-punch, stamps)
- `sets/` (practice, stage, rooftop, orbit, math, sky, void, datacenter)
- `fx/` (flood, fold, burn, iris, window, rip)
- `inserts/` (photocopy generator)
- `shots.js` (this shot list as data)
- `studio.html` (scrub, beat ticks, onion skin, plate overlay)

**QA loop**
- For every render: a contact sheet at every beat, and a "sync scope" debug render that draws kick, clap and syllable ticks onto the frames.
- Claude reviews each sheet against section 4 before any full render.
- Watch the full film at least 3 times per iteration.

- **Budget.** About 150 s per pass. At 3 takes on average ≈ 450 s x $0.30 ≈ **$135**, plus about 200 images (≈ $10–40). That is about 10% of the stated fal credit.
- **Non-human characters need no plates:** Subagents, Shoggoth, Sydney and the others are procedural.

**A5. Sync verification loop (automatic; runs per take)**
1. Decode the take to PNGs at exactly 24 fps (`-vf fps=24`). This Chromium has no H.264 decoder, so image sequences are required.
2. **Lip-sync check (PL01–04, 07, 09–11):**
   - MediaPipe FaceLandmarker gives the `jawOpen` blendshape per frame; smooth it with a One Euro filter.
   - The reference is the vocal clip's RMS envelope resampled to 24 fps, plus song.json syllable onsets as an impulse train.
   - (a) Cross-correlate over ±12 frames: **accept if the best lag is within ±1 frame** and r ≥ 0.4.
   - (b) **Syllable hit rate:** at least 75% of syllable onsets must have a jawOpen rise (a positive derivative peak) within ±2 frames.
   - A constant lag is fixed by shifting `lag_frames`. Otherwise regenerate, up to 3 takes.
3. **Dance check (PL05, 06, 08, 12–14):**
   - PoseLandmarker gives hip-centre vertical velocity plus wrist speed, turned into a motion-onset curve.
   - Cross-correlate with the kick and clap grid from song.json `hits[]`. Accept a lag of ±1 frame or less.
   - If the take drifts, **time-warp it**: a DTW between motion onsets and the beat grid, constrained to ±3 frames, then piecewise-linear retiming of frame indices.
   - Because plates are only reference, retiming is free and invisible. This is the key de-risking move.
4. **Mouths are always ours.** Even with a perfect plate, the drawn mouth is driven by the song.json viseme track (5B). Plate `jawOpen` only scales the amplitude. Sync is therefore guaranteed by construction, and plates contribute head motion, body acting and timing feel.
5. **Human check.** A 3-up sync contact sheet (plate frame | our drawing | waveform with syllable ticks) at every syllable onset of each hook, reviewed by Claude before any rotoscoping continues.

**A6. Rotoscoping plates into JS**
1. **Track** (CPU, about 0.4 s/frame at 1080p [M, craft]). Output: `roto/PLxx.json` per frame.
   - PoseLandmarker (33 points, VIDEO mode)
   - HandLandmarker (21 points x 2; the dial needs fingers)
   - FaceLandmarker (478 points, 52 blendshapes, head pose matrix)
   - the multiclass selfie segmenter (hair / body / face / clothes)
2. **Smooth** with One Euro filters. **Sample on twos** (ones in chorus 4 and the outro, threes in the breakdown and chair).
3. **Retarget angles, not positions.** Compute joint rotations (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, neck, head yaw/pitch/roll) and apply them to the idol rig's fixed bone lengths. The idol is 6.5 heads with a big halo, not the plate's proportions.
   - Root position comes from the hip centre, scaled to the set.
   - Hands snap to the nearest pose in the hand library if the landmark confidence is below 0.6.
4. **Silhouette assist.** Segmentation masks go through potrace (esm-potrace-wasm) to simplified polygons, which guide the skirt and sleeve cut-out shapes per drawing. Each is re-torn per boil variant.
5. **Secondary motion is ours.** The halo petals, skirt pleats and scarf are springs driven by the rig's head and hip acceleration. Plates can't do paper physics.
6. **Animator pass.** Claude reviews the onion-skinned studio view and edits keys where MediaPipe fails (occlusions, spins, hands). Every correction is stored as an override JSON on top of the tracked data, so re-tracking never destroys hand edits.
7. **Invisible plates.** `studio.html` can show a plate at 30% under the drawing for review. The render path never loads plates.

### 5.B Pure-JS mode (the default until fal is confirmed)

**B1. The idol rig**
- About 22 cut-out parts: halo disc with 12 petal sprites, hair back, bob, bangs, face card, eyes (per-state sprites), brows, mouth (viseme set), neck, torso (per era), 2-segment arms, hands (library), skirt (6 pleat panels), 2-segment legs and shoes.
- A 2D bone hierarchy with per-bone pivots. It is a flat puppet in the Spider-Verse and cut-out-animation tradition, deliberately not a smooth deformer.
- Face features are hand-tuned bezier ink ribbons. This is where most design iteration goes.

**B2. Choreography**
- A **pose library**: DIAL_0–5, BLOOM, HOCKEY_A/B, V_EYE, PLEA, FINGER_HEART, RUNWAY_L/R, RUNNING_MAN_1–4, BOW, CHAIR_SLUMP, FLOAT, SYNC_A/B, DISOBEY, RIBBON_1–4, PANT.
- **8-count sheets** in JSON (count = 8th note) map counts to poses with an ease (backOut for hits, sine for sways).
- Weight shifts are authored explicitly: a hip offset toward the standing leg on every step, a 2-frame anticipation before every hit.
- Formation morphs use Hungarian assignment and 1-beat eases.

**B3. Lip-sync from data**
- Split every word into phonemes with the analysis lexicon (`analysis/tools/lyrics_lex.py`, ARPAbet). Map them to 10 visemes.
- Place phoneme times inside each word by typical-duration ratios, snapped so vowels start on the syllable onsets from song.json.
- Held notes hold the vowel viseme, with a 2 px jaw tremble on vibrato.
- Mouths change on twos but always land on the onset frame.

**B4. Building shots procedurally (one builder per shot type)**

| Shot type | Built from | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ECU face | idol face at 4x with dedicated high-detail eye sprites; iris glyph animation | S00, S16, S69, S77 |
| MS / MCU performance | rig + set layers + S/M type | verse shots |
| Stage wide | stage set (5 baked layers), rig, 8 Subagent instances, crowd strips (3 baked parallax strips with per-row wave offsets), LED halftone panels (shader) | choruses |
| Top-down formation | plan-view sprites (top-of-head, halo disc; Subagent tops), formation engine | S17, S33, S34, S75 |
| Full-bleed type | fontkit outlines, split-flap engine, weaving mask, anagram solver | S20, S21, S66 |
| Insert (photocopy) | vector UI → 1-bit threshold + toner noise + tape | throughout |
| Accumulation | instanced sprites with deterministic spawn times, baked in 1-second chunks to an offscreen canvas | paperclips, collage pile-up, SOLVED stamps |
| FX | shader burn, fold (split sprite with scaleX = cos θ plus a shade gradient), tear (two sprites sharing a torn edge), ink flood (radial threshold of noise) | transitions |

**B5. What is hardest (ranked)**
1. **Her appeal.** A charming, non-Pixar, non-generic idol face in cut paper and ink is make-or-break. Budget the most iteration here: build an expression editor, render 50 face variants, and pick with the client.
2. **Dance with weight in a flat rig.** Without plates, K-pop choreography can look stiff. Mitigations:
   - prioritise upper-body point moves;
   - use formation travel and camera for energy;
   - hold hits on twos with overshoot;
   - never show a full-body complex move for longer than 2 bars in pure-JS mode.
3. **Hands in THE DIAL.** Readable finger poses at MCU need dedicated high-resolution hand sprites.
4. **The pile-up (S70) and the great rip (S71).** It needs a curated library of about 60 inserts, all built and tested. The rip must read in 3 frames.
5. **Crowd scale in chorus 4** (thousands doing the dial). Pre-render the crowd strips as 6-frame loops per dial click.
6. **Tempo of iteration.** Keep a 960x540 preview under 0.5 s/frame, so a 10 s shot previews in under 2 minutes.
