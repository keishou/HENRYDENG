# OUT OF REGISTER: a riso-zine music video for "I'm Upping My P(doom)" (Claude Pop)

**Treatment: the zine angle.** Director's treatment, 2026-09-28. Song: `pdoom.mp3`, 156.65 s, 132 BPM, 4/4, 86 bars (untouched; never re-encoded for the master).

**How to read the timings.**
- Every time comes from `claudepop/analysis/song.json`. Beat k = 0.235 + k × 0.454545 s. Bar n starts at 0.235 + (n − 1) × 1.818182 s.
- **The master is 60 fps** (section 3.8 explains why: the drawing rate of this film climbs from 3.75 to 60 drawings per second). A sound at time t shows on frame **f = floor(60 t)**, the frame on screen when the sound hits. Visual hits land on that frame, never later. Where it helps, 24 fps frame numbers (floor(24 t)) are given too.
- "Hold H" means one drawing is held for H frames at 60 fps, so the drawing rate is 60/H drawings per second (dps). ∞ means frozen.
- Word and syllable times are the refined ones from song.json (hook syllables ±30 ms; most words ±50–150 ms).

## 0. The idea in one paragraph

The video is a hand-made **riso zine** that comes alive: a K-pop comeback album the size of a paperback, printed in fluorescent riso inks, and we thumb through it from the front cover to the back. Its cover star is **CLAUDE ✻**, a hinged paper-puppet idol with a halo of coral paper petals. Her backup dancers are her own **colour separations**: three single-ink copies of her (Pink, Blue, Yellow) that step out of her body on every drop.

Three paper metaphors carry the whole song:

1. **Registration = alignment.** When the three ink plates line up, the picture is crisp: *aligned*. From the first "AGI" (4.33 s), when the plates snap into register, they drift further apart with every chorus as the P(doom) wheel climbs. At "RLHF goes askew" a plate literally rotates 7°. By the finale her separations dance as independent beings. SF tech Twitter will read "the misregistration is the misalignment" within a second.
2. **Frame rate = takeoff.** The zine starts as a hand-flipped flip-book at **3.75 drawings per second** and doubles its drawing rate section by section: 7.5 → 15 → 30 → **60**. The bridge doubles it every two bars, "the takeoff" felt in the viewer's eyeballs. The final chorus runs on true 60 fps ones, uncannily smooth, while the crowd's paper-pinwheel lightsticks spin faster than the frame rate and appear to stop and run backward (the wagon-wheel effect: speed beyond what the medium can sample). Then "Was it all for show?" plays on a single held drawing: **0 fps**.
3. **Print run = scale.** A risograph duplicator, the "Press", prints faster every chorus. Its pages-per-minute dial rises from 60 to 150, pins in the red and snaps off. Copies become agents, and by the outro 10,000 printer's devils fill the page.

Every lyric becomes a **paper machine** or **paper mechanism**:
- a cootie-catcher oracle that eats a card catalog of math problems (Navier–Stokes included)
- a dot-matrix printout that shoots vertical like a party streamer on FOOM
- a gatefold that opens because "singularity's" is too long for the page, and a die-cut hole through the whole book as the event horizon
- a split-flap board flipping SO BACK / SO OVER faster and faster
- a toploader she is trapped in, a lace-papercut basilisk, a stock ticker whose tape rolls into a rocket
- the photocopier, where the half-time chorus degrades one xerox generation per bar
- a house of transformer cards, a Jacquard loom foretelling RACE / SLOWDOWN
- an advent-calendar door labelled ILYA, taped shut

Lyrics are never subtitles pasted on top: they are **printed, stamped, typed, cut, folded, torn and flapped** into the paper world.

### 1.1 Logline

> A paper K-pop idol printed on a runaway risograph: every chorus the press runs faster, the zine's frame rate doubles, and her ink plates drift further out of register, until the whole print run explodes and the last page asks one quiet question: *was it all for show?*

### 1.2 Why this wins on SF tech Twitter

1. **A thesis you can state in one post.** "The frame rate doubles as the singularity approaches, and the misregistration is the misalignment." That is quote-post bait for nerds. Both metaphors are technically true of the render, so replies like "wait, it actually goes 7.5 → 15 → 30 → 60 fps" do the marketing. A tiny folio in the corner of every page ("p.07 ▸ 15 fps") lets the attentive see it happen.
2. **Handmade in the year of "slop"** (Merriam-Webster's 2025 word of the year [H]). Riso ink, torn paper, brass paper fasteners and boiling pencil lines read as anti-slop at thumbnail size. The colophon's claim, "hand-drawn in JavaScript, no generated pixels printed", is true in both production modes.
3. **Instant Claude recognition without a logo.** The coral petal halo reads as the spark [H], and the cover's corner shows a Claude Code spinner line cycling `· ✢ ✳ ✶ ✻ ✽` in sync with her petals opening. The audience clocks it in the first second.
4. **K-pop grammar the fandom-literate will enjoy.** The zine *is* a K-pop album package: photocards, toploaders, fold-out poster, fan slogan towels, a comeback teaser poster ("COMING SOON · 6PM KST"), eye-smiles, center formation, a repeating point move (CRANK & BLOOM), and a "Congratulations" ring curtain call. The album versions are "PINK ver. / BLUE ver. / YELLOW ver." after her separations. KPop Demon Hunters proved in 2025 that a stylised, non-Pixar animated girl group is mass currency (Netflix's most-watched film, 266 M views by 2025-09-03 [H]).
5. **Muted autoplay is solved by design.** Frame 0 is already a lyric poster. Every sung line exists as burned-in type in one of four tiers, and the big lines are set pieces, not captions.
6. **Pause-bait at the right depth.** Each is a screenshot and a reply:
   - the "+ f" circled in red pencil on the Navier–Stokes index card
   - the Jacobian counterexample typed on a strip ("det J = −2")
   - the "grokking?" pencil note on the loss curve
   - a "YOU HAVE 18 MONTHS TO ESCAPE THE PERMANENT UNDERCLASS" flyer with tear-off tabs

7. **Speed you feel, then silence you hear.** Cut rate, drawing rate, press speed, stamp rate and type density all accelerate together. The peak (136.6–138.4 s) is too dense to read on purpose. It slams shut into one typewritten line on blank paper, the only fully still image in the film.
8. **Both camps can repost it.** The idol is charming and pro-Claude. The song is about doom. The ending is ambiguous (in register, then one plate slips 2 px).

## 2. Protagonist and cast

### 2.1 CLAUDE ✻ (클로드), the cover star

**Concept.** A hinged paper puppet: cut card parts joined by **brass split-pin paper fasteners (brads)**, visible as small gold dots at every joint. It is the oldest animation rig there is (Lotte Reiniger's cut-paper films, 1920s [K]) worn by a 2026 K-pop idol. She is designed to be rotoscope-ready: every body part is a rigid piece with a single pivot, so either a tracked dancer or hand-keyed angles can drive her.

**Not Pixar, explicitly.**
- No volume, no subsurface skin, no rim light, no baby proportions, no big glossy eyes.
- Tone is printed (halftone dots and flat ink), never shaded.
- Her beauty comes from the **drawing**: a brush-ink calligraphic face on flat card, proportioned like fashion illustration and manhwa rather than a toy.

**Fallback name** if Anthropic will not sign off on "Claude": **HAE ✻ (해, "sun")**. Nothing else in the design changes.

#### Silhouette (the thumbnail test)
- **A sunflower on a stem.** The halo (flower head) reads as a sunburst. Her tall, slim 7-head figure with a narrow jacket and flared pleated skirt is the stem and leaves.
- **Blunt black bob with straight bangs** (black card) framing a cream face. This gives maximum contrast against any stock and reads at 40 px tall.
- **Halo: 12 tapered, round-ended coral petals**, each on its own brad behind the head:
  - Lengths vary ±12%, angles irregular by ±4°, so it reads hand-cut and spark-like without copying the Claude logo.
  - A second, inner ring of 12 short **Sunflower-yellow** (#FFB511) petals, offset half a petal, peeks through when fully open.
- **Bloom stages S0–S5** mirror the Claude Code spinner `· ✢ ✳ ✶ ✻ ✽`: 0/20/40/60/80/100% open. Bud (S0–S1) in quiet verses, full bloom on every DOOM.

#### Face (replacement pieces, like stop-motion replacement faces but paper)
- **Base.** Cream card (#F4EEE2 printed at 8% Coral for skin warmth). A single ink-tick nose. Thin straight K-beauty brows.
- **Eyes.** Almond shaped, with a thick upper lash line and a short wing (brush ink). A thin under-eye line with a white gouache highlight (aegyo-sal). **Coral irises carrying a registration crosshair ⊕**, with a tiny ✻ at the pupil. Two white gouache catchlight dots.
- **Eye replacements (7):** open, half, closed, **eye-smile crescent (눈웃음)**, wide, ⊕-locked (targeting), spiral (the bliss/shrooms moment).
- **Mouths (9 replacements):** rest / M-B-P closed, A, E, I, O, U, F-V, L-TH, open smile. Small; the upper lip is a single ink stroke.
- **Blush:** two Fluorescent Pink halftone ovals, always 3 px misregistered: a signature imperfection.
- **Face gem:** one fluoro-pink star sticker on the left cheekbone.
- **Earrings:** registration marks ⊕ on short chains, which swing on a spring. A small black binder clip in her hair.

#### Outfit (paper-native couture)
The outfit is inspired by Issey Miyake's **132 5.** line, garments that fold flat from origami patterns [K]. It is a nice rhyme with a 2-D puppet, and with a 132 BPM song.
- **Cropped jacket.** Crisp folded card in **Blue #0078BF** with pleated, sharp origami shoulders, printed with a 45° fine-line screen.
- **Skirt.** An **accordion-pleated A-line mini** in **Fluorescent Pink #FF48B0**. Its 12 pleats are a real accordion fold that compresses on crouches and fans open on spins.
- **Belt.** A **chain of large silver paperclips** (foreshadowing chorus 3).
- **Boots.** Black knee-high card boots with a 3 px white torn core rim.
- **Headset mic.** A thin ink line: a K-pop stage headset.
- **Re-inking = outfit change.** A zine can reprint the same drawing in different inks for free, so her "stage outfits" per section are ink swaps:
  - verses: Blue / Pink
  - pre-choruses: white knockouts on black
  - chorus 2: Yellow jacket
  - chorus 3: toner black
  - finale: Metallic Gold (#AC936E) jacket

#### Construction (14 rigid parts + extras)
- **Body:** head, neck, torso, pelvis + skirt, 2 upper arms, 2 forearms, 2 hands, 2 thighs, 2 shins + boots.
- **Replacement heads:** 3 (front, ¾ left/right mirrored, profile). The halo is a separate layer that foreshortens as an ellipse with head yaw.
- **Replacement hands:** 8: fist, open, point, V, finger-heart, stamp-palm, crank-grip, scissors.
- **Petals:** 12 + 12 on springs.

#### How she moves
- **Snap and settle.** She moves in poses. Each hit gets one overshoot drawing (+5–8° past the target) and settles on the next. Paper does not stretch, so squash is done with replacement drawings (a crouch torso, a jump torso), never by scaling.
- **Paper weight.** Jumps rise at normal speed and flutter down at about 1.2× the airtime, with a slight lateral sway, like a sheet falling.
- **Secondary motion.** Petals lag one drawing behind the head and overshoot. The skirt pleat angle follows hip rotation speed. Earrings swing.
- **Rhythm.**
  - Choruses: she hits the kick with the torso and the clap with the head.
  - Verse 1 has no drums: she moves on the pluck 8ths (small shoulder ticks).
- **Eye contact:** to camera on every line's first downbeat (the K-pop "killing" glance).

#### Signature gestures
1. **CRANK & BLOOM** (the point move; every hook; loop L1).
   - "I'm | up | ping | my | P": her right fist cranks one small circle beside her right ear per syllable (8th notes; quarter notes in chorus 4). Her head ratchets 4° per crank, and her halo opens one stage per crank (S0 → S4).
   - "DOOM": both hands fling open from her temples with fingers splayed. They become two extra rays of the sunburst: **sunburst hands**, halo at S5.
   - On the same drawing, the three separations step out of her body on three vectors: **SEPARATE**.
   - It is upper-body only, so it reads at thumbnail size.
2. **STAMP.** A palm-down press on the beat, like inking a rubber stamp. Used for the 28 outro stamps.
3. **EYE-V.** A V-sign across one eye: the idol and purikura classic, used on "see" lines.
4. **FLIP.** A thumb flicking the edge of an invisible flip-book beside her cheek. Used in the bridge, where the drawing rate doubles.

### 2.2 Backup dancers: THE SEPARATIONS (Pink "806", Blue "3005", Yellow "Y")

- **What they are.** Three copies of CLAUDE, each a full-figure **monochrome halftone proof** of her in one ink (Fluorescent Pink, Blue, Yellow). Their "member numbers" are their Pantone ink references, printed on their jackets: **806, 3005, Y**. The Yellow sep gets a thin pencil keyline so she reads on cream.
- **Mechanic.**
  - When all three stand exactly on her, in register, they vanish into her full-colour print.
  - On every DOOM they **step out** of her body (Pink left, Blue right, Yellow up/back).
  - Their distance from her follows the film's **registration arc** (section 3.8): 2 px in chorus 1, 40 px by chorus 4.
  - In the chorus-3 xerox they are **gone**: a photocopy has no colour plates, so she is alone. On "blues" they walk back into her one by one.
  - In the outro they dance as independent beings. On the final kick they snap back into her.
- **Formations** (K-pop centre rules; morphs on 2- or 4-bar boundaries, eased over 1 beat):
  - **line**
  - **V**
  - **orbit**: 120° apart, stepping round one position per beat
  - **canon**: the same move one 8th apart, Pink → Blue → Yellow
  - **the misregistration stack**: all three directly behind her at small offsets, a formation only a print can have

### 2.3 Crew and crowd

- **THE PRINTER'S DEVILS (×8, later ×10,000).** "Printer's devil" is the old name for a print-shop apprentice [K].
  - Build: small orange cardstock blocks (Riso Orange #FF6C2F), wider than tall, with two hole-punched eyes (you see whatever is behind them) and four **accordion-folded paper-spring legs** that bounce.
  - Hands: black **binder-clip claws**. A pencil stub is tucked on top. Numbered badges 001–008.
  - They crank, feed, stamp, wheatpaste and shred.
  - They are inspired by Clawd, the Claude Code mascot, but are a distinct design (binder-clip claws, spring legs, punched eyes); brand sign-off is still advised.
  - In the outro, 10,000 of them (one baked sprite, instanced) are the "10,000 agents".
- **THE FANS.** A 1-bit photocopied crowd strip, repeated with offsets.
  - They hold **paper-pinwheel lightsticks**: 8-blade spark-shaped pinwheels in Coral, Pink and Yellow, whose spin rate is the song's speedometer.
  - For the three "please" prayers (ChatGPT, Sydney, Gato) they raise a **fan slogan towel** with the name, always at the bottom-left, same size and layout: a structural rhyme.

## 3. Style bible

### 3.1 The look in one line

**A riso zine come alive:** fluorescent spot inks overprinting multiplicatively on uncoated cream stock, with halftone mid-tones, visible misregistration, starved-ink edges and paper tooth. Hand-cut paper puppets are joined by brass brads and cast soft real shadows. Pencil and brush-ink linework boils on the drawing clock. Everything on screen is something that could be **printed, cut, folded, taped or stamped**, including the type and the internet inserts (which enter as taped-in photocopies, never as crisp RGB screenshots).

**Lineage** (for the client, not to copy):
- Lotte Reiniger's cut-paper silhouettes [K]; Saul Bass cut paper, via the 2017 "Look What You Made Me Do" lyric video [S]; Terry Gilliam collage cut-outs [K]
- Spider-Verse's print artefacts [S]; The Mitchells vs. the Machines' handmade AI apocalypse [S]
- Kijek/Adamski's papercraft MV for Shugo Tokumaru's "Katachi" (2013) [S]
- NewJeans × Powerpuff Girls (idols as flat 2-D graphics) [S]
- riso zine culture [K]

### 3.3 Paper materials and textures

- **One sheet per drawing, not a filter.** The paper texture lives in *world* space on each sheet, so it moves with the camera and each layer, unlike the previous video's screen-locked overlay. The **grain inside a sheet is static** (real paper does not shimmer). Life comes from the boil. This also protects X's re-encode.
- **Riso artefacts,** all in the shader:
  - halftone AM dots on a rotated screen (cell ≥ 10 px at 1080p, screens fixed per ink: Pink 75°, Blue 15°, Yellow 0°)
  - solids with **tooth-thresholded edges** (ink starvation)
  - starved-ink speckle
  - faint **roller streaks** along the feed direction
  - **set-off**: a ghost of the previous sheet's ink on fresh prints in the pressroom
  - per-plate **misregistration offsets and rotation** driven by the registration arc
- **Cut edges.**
  - Scissor-cut: a clean, slightly faceted polyline with a 1–2 px white core.
  - Torn: fibrous, with a displaced white rim 3–6 px wide. Tears are reserved for violent beats (the DOOM rips, "boom", "safety fence").
- **Shadows.** Each paper layer casts a baked soft shadow, offset (0.35, 0.55) × layer height, warm black at 25–35%. Pop-ups and gatefolds cast moving shadows (a shadow sprite skewed with the fold angle).
- **Fasteners and adhesives.** Brass brads (gold dot, a highlight that glints on hits), staples (the centerfold gutter), masking tape, washi strips, glue shine on wheatpasted posters.
- **Other stock.** Acetate (toploader), glassine, silver foil (mirror), corrugated card edges (printer's devils' bodies show corrugation on their cut sides), index-card stock with a red top rule and blue lines.

**Motion rules (hard).**
1. **Type never fades, glides or glows.** It is *printed* (1-drawing ink-in: scale 1.06 → 1.00, ink-spread threshold 0.40 → 0.62), *stamped* (squash plus double-print ghost), *typed* (per character, carriage jolt), *cut* (a blade visibly travels), *pasted*, *torn*, *folded*, *flapped* (split-flap) or *popped up*.
2. **A word appears on the drawing that contains its onset.** Events reset the drawing clock (section 3.8), so even at 3.75 dps a word lands on floor(60 t).
3. **A printed word stays** until a paper action removes it (a tear, turn, cover or fold).
4. **At most one L/XL event per beat,** except the hook syllables (8ths) and the NVDA / GPU / RLHF / PTO letter runs (8ths).
5. **Calm space.** An L/XL word gets ≥ 35% of the frame as quiet paper. The idol sits on the opposite third, or small.
6. **Type obeys registration.** Its plates misregister exactly as the world does (section 3.8).
7. **Contrast for feed.** Dark ink on light stock, or knockout (paper showing through black). No saturated-on-saturated small type, which H.264 4:2:0 smears.

### 3.6 Camera grammar

- **A rostrum camera over a physical zine.** It is a down-shooter looking at stacked paper layers (4–6 planes) with real parallax and cast shadows. It **moves on the drawing clock**: at 7.5 dps a push-in advances in 7.5 steps per second, like a real stop-motion camera. From 60 dps (bridge bars 67–68 onward) the camera is finally smooth, which is part of the takeoff.
- **Moves:**
  - (a) Flat-lay wide: the whole spread, gutter staples visible in the centerfold.
  - (b) Push-in to a detail, at most one per bar.
  - (c) Tilt along long objects (printout, card tower, loom threads).
  - (d) Through-hole push: into punched eyes, zoetrope slits, die-cut tunnels, door cracks.
  - (e) Table-bump: a 1-drawing 4–8 px jolt on chorus kicks and on the verse-3 crash (74.78).
  - (f) Rotation only when the *paper* rotates (turning the zine 90° at a section change, or the "askew" plate rotation). Never a Dutch tilt for its own sake.
- **Composition.**
  - K-pop centre: in every chorus wide the idol stands on the page gutter, the literal centre of the spread.
  - Lyric-left / idol-right for hero lines; full-bleed type with a small idol for slow lines.
  - One calm quadrant in every frame.
  - The eye rhyme: four extreme close-ups of her ⊕ iris (hook 4.8 s, shinigami 33.3 s, Ilya 132.9 s, ending 153 s).
- **Portrait (9:16).** A zine page is naturally portrait: **16:9 shows the spread, 9:16 shows one page.** The vertical cut reflows each spread into its two pages stacked in time (lyric page, then idol page) or picks the idol page and moves type into its upper third. ### 3.8 How "speeding up" is felt

**The drawing clock** (the core engine rule):
```
e      = last event at or before t   (beats; in verse 1 also pluck 8ths; every word/syllable onset that prints type)
dps    = drawingRate(t)              (table below)
k      = floor((t - e.t) * dps)      (drawing index inside the event)
tDraw  = e.t + k / dps               (the "shutter time" of the current drawing)
render(scene, tDraw, seed = hash(e.id, k))
```
Every event starts a fresh drawing exactly on its frame, so sync never depends on the hold length. Output is always 60 fps. Held drawings are copied, not re-rendered.

# tools/cut_audio.py

PLATES = {"P03": (16.144, 31.144, "mix"), "P02": (5.235, 16.599, "vocal")}   # … all 14

```

#### A.4 Verification loop (`tools/verify_plate.py`), run on every generation

1. Probe fps and duration (Seedance output is likely 24 fps [K]). Duration must be ≥ the window.
2. Decode to a PNG sequence. This Chromium has no H.264 decoder, so plates are always image sequences.
3. Run MediaPipe **FaceLandmarker** in VIDEO mode to get per-frame blendshapes (`jawOpen`, `mouthClose`, `mouthFunnel`, `mouthPucker`, `mouthSmile*`, `eyeBlink*`). Run **PoseLandmarker** (18–45 ms/frame on this CPU [M]) and **HandLandmarker**.
4. From the matching stem, compute a 20 ms-hop RMS envelope and its positive derivative (onset strength), resampled to the plate fps.
5. **Global lag.** Cross-correlate the positive derivative of `jawOpen` with vocal onset strength over ±12 frames. Take the lag τ and correlation r.
6. **Syllable hits.** For every song.json word and syllable onset inside the window, look for a `jawOpen` rising edge within ±2 frames after τ is applied. Report the hit rate. Hook syllables are listed separately.
7. **Cue hits** (dance). For each cue, look for a wrist-speed or hip-velocity peak within ±2 frames. The five cranks must show five wrist-circle phases.
8. **Accept** only if all hold:
   - |τ| ≤ 3 frames (a constant shift is fixed by re-mapping), with a residual ≤ 1 frame (42 ms)
   - r ≥ 0.45
   - syllable hit rate ≥ 80%, and hook syllables 5/5
   - cue hit rate ≥ 80%
9. Write a report PNG (jawOpen against the envelope, with cue markers) and a 10 s side-by-side preview with audio for a human glance.
10. **On reject:** regenerate with a new seed (at most 3 attempts), then fall back to mode B for that shot. The film never waits on a plate.

#### A.5 Rotoscoping plates into the JS puppet (`tools/solve_rig.py` → `rig/P##.json`)

- **Root.** Mid-hip position and scale (hip-to-shoulder distance normalised to the puppet torso), plus torso angle.
- **Limbs.** Upper and lower arm and leg angles from atan2 of landmark pairs (11–16, 23–28), a 2-D solve. Where limbs foreshorten, pick the nearest **replacement** piece (straight / bent / foreshortened).
- **Head.**
  - Roll from the eye line.
  - Yaw from the nose-to-cheek ratio, which chooses the replacement head (front / ¾ / profile) with hysteresis.
  - The halo ellipse is foreshortened by the yaw.
- **Mouth.** Blendshapes map to the 9 visemes through a rule table. **Hybrid:** the plate says *when* and *how wide*, and song.json's lyric text says *which vowel*. Hook syllables are hand-checked.
- **Eyes.** Blinks from `eyeBlink*`. The eye-smile fires when `eyeSquint` and `mouthSmile` both exceed thresholds.
- **Hands.** The 21 hand landmarks are matched to the nearest of the 8 hand templates by fingertip-extension pattern.
- **Smoothing.** One Euro filter (min cutoff 1.0, beta 0.02) that is **split at cue frames**, so hits are never smeared.
- **Resampling.** Angles are spline-sampled at the drawing clock's `tDraw`. The same plate therefore drives 3.75 dps or 60 dps, and 60 dps in-betweens come from interpolated angles rather than from traced frames.
- **Puppet grammar on top.**
  - An overshoot drawing is added at each cue.
  - Petals, pleats and earrings are procedural secondary motion.
  - Brad glints appear on hits.
- **Debug only.** `?underlay=P03` shows the plate at 30% under the puppet in the studio page. It never appears in the render.

```
- In-betweens use eased interpolation. Every cue gets an overshoot drawing. Holds follow the rate table.
- The Separations replay the same track with canon offsets and mirroring. The devils have 4 cycles (walk, crank, stamp, carry). The crowd is a 1-bit strip with per-figure phase offsets.

**Lip-sync from the lyric data.**
- Every word already has an onset, and hooks and letter runs have syllables. Map each syllable's vowel to a viseme using a 220-word hand-made lexicon for the 46 lines, and add M/B/P closures on those consonants.
- Open on the onset and close about 60 ms before the next consonant.
- Held notes ("Sydney" 53.0–55.9, "know" 134.4–136.6, "blues" 107.5–108.3) hold the vowel with a jaw flutter keyed on the drawing clock.
- Wordless ad-libs get "ah"/"oo" shapes on the `vocal_extra` spans.

**What is hardest in pure JS, and how to beat it**
1. **Her face and appeal.** Everything rests on it. Draw the face and replacement sets as hand-authored ink paths. Iterate at least 4 rounds of render → phone-size review → fix before any shot work. Style frames SF1/SF2, if image generation is allowed even just for reference, set the target.
2. **K-pop dance without reference motion.** Design around what reads on a puppet: **point moves**, held poses, formation morphs and zoetrope loops. The low drawing rates of the early sections turn stepped motion into a style instead of a flaw. The full-body dance is concentrated in chorus 4 and the outro (60 dps), where the hand-keyed poses need the most in-betweens: budget the most authoring time there.
3. **The `Sheet` primitive.** Page turns, gatefolds, pop-ups and folds need one solid 2.5-D paper renderer. Build it first and reuse it everywhere.
4. **Density that still composes** (chorus 4, the peak at 136.6–138.4). Use a strict layer budget: one hero object, one hero word, one collage layer, plus the idol.
5. **Volume.** 58 shots, about 15 mechanisms, about 190 type events. Generating `typeEvents.json` from song.json removes the hand-timing risk. The shot files are small because the modules carry the weight.

### 6.1 Risks and mitigations

| # | Risk | Mitigation |

| 1 | **The stepped early motion (7.5 dps) reads as "laggy" to scrollers** before they understand the ladder. | Every word lands on its frame.
