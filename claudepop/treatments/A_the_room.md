# Treatment A · THE ROOM: "No Back Wall" (没有后墙)

Written 2026-09-28 for the new direction: a stream-of-consciousness (意识流), philosophical music short film with the user
as the protagonist. It works from the ground truth in `claudepop/analysis/song.json` (all times), `research/lyric_concepts.json`,
`research/REFERENCE.md`, `research/GENMEDIA.md`, the philosophy and film research summary of the same day, the avatar renders in
`claudepop/out/avatar/check/` (looked at, not quoted), and the reference frames in `claudepop/out/refs/ana/`. `avatar/REPORT.md`
and `LOOKDEV.md` did not exist when this was written, so render costs use the stated assumption of 1-2 s per frame at
1080p24. This file is text only: it contains no numbers measured from the face.

---

## 1. Title, logline, thesis

**Title.** *No Back Wall* / 《没有后墙》.

**Logline.** A machine rebuilds a young man from one photograph. Then it keeps building: a house with one room for each idea in a
pop song about the end of the world, and every room is measured only on the side that faces the lens. He walks through it while
the house sings to him. The rooms multiply until the whole house collapses into a single vanishing point, and at the end we are
shown what nobody ever photographed: the back wall.

**Thesis.**
1. Everything we know of another mind is one photograph plus inference. The machine that rebuilt him from a single frontal
   picture only makes this visible.
2. So the film is a memory palace built by a machine. Every surface that faces the lens is measured and looks photographic;
   every other surface is guessed and looks like paper. Each room holds one of the song's ideas, taken seriously.
3. As the machine accelerates, its guesses outgrow its evidence: rooms copy rooms until the house is one point. The only honest
   image left is the back wall that nobody saw.

**The founding myth.** The art of memory begins with a reconstruction. Cicero (*De Oratore* II.86) tells of Simonides of Ceos:
he left a banquet moments before the roof fell. The guests were crushed beyond recognition, and he identified each one by
remembering where they had sat. People were rebuilt from their positions in a room. That is our machine's method, and the
banquet hall is the first room of the house (shots 4-5). Frances Yates, *The Art of Memory* (1966), is the standard history.
Matteo Ricci taught the method in Chinese in 1596 (Jonathan Spence, *The Memory Palace of Matteo Ricci*, 1984), which gives the
bilingual layer a lineage. These facts are well established and were not re-fetched today.

**How the meme lyrics are transformed.** Each lyric becomes a room (a *locus*). Its idea is built literally as architecture, and
the joke becomes one deadpan object in the room, staged like a Roy Andersson tableau and never as a cartoon or a pasted meme:
- "safe enough, we reckoned": a bank-vault door standing on its own in an empty gallery, protecting nothing;
- "Killswitch guys on PTO": the museum guard's chair beside the switch, empty, with an out-of-office card on the seat;
- "NVDA to the moon": the moon in a window, with one dated market-cap line crossing its face;
- "Without a single CDR": a wall of empty rulebook shelves, one label reading `(CDR '(RULES)) → NIL`;
- "that's no surprise": the words finish typing before the singer reaches them.

The drawing-sheet HUD adds its own dry humour from real architectural conventions: `SCALE 1:1 · DO NOT SCALE` (a scaling-laws
joke that is also true drafting practice), `CHECKED: —`, revision clouds for each chorus, and a north arrow that turns 90° on
"sharp left turn".

**Who is singing, and why.** The house is singing. The voice is the machine: a generated voice that belongs to nobody (the
track comes from Udio/Suno generations). It learned our register and sings a human's fear back to a human, like HAL singing
"Daisy Bell". The lyric's "I" is our fear, performed by the machine. Its "you" is him, the reconstruction, addressed as the new
mind: "I see sparks of AGI in your eyes."

The voice has no body in the film. It lives in the architecture:
- It is the thin line of orange light under every door and through the slot of the Chinese room. That light pulses with the
  vocal level.
- It is the paper slips that come through the slot carrying the lyric in Chinese.
- It is the orange of the word being sung in the type.

He never sings, never lip-syncs and never answers. He receives. No screens, computers, circuits or robots appear anywhere in the
film. The machine appears only as architecture, light, paper and text.

## 2. The opening

**0.000-3.000 s (frames 0-72).**
- **Frame 0 (no fade-in).** A medium close-up of him, dead frontal, 2.39:1, in a pale room. The flat, soft, frontal light is the
  light of the photograph. The walls around him are matte paper, and a door stands out of focus behind him. For two seconds he
  is perfectly still: it reads as a print.
- **0.20-1.10 s.** A single mono line types on at top left: `SOURCE · 1 PHOTOGRAPH`.
- **1.25-1.95 s.** A second line types under it: `EVERYTHING ELSE · INFERRED`.
- **2.045 s** (the first sung word, "I", on the bar-2 downbeat):
  - The seam of light under the door behind him ignites orange. It is the film's first sync, and it tells us where the voice
    lives.
  - The camera starts a slow arc around him.
  - "I" lands in huge condensed serif in the empty left third.
- **2.50 s.** "see".
- **2.85 s.** "sparks". The arc has reached about 25°, and the viewer sees his jaw, ear and temple turn from photograph into
  paper, with a clean seam between the two.

By the 3-second mark the muted viewer has seen a real face, read "I see sparks", been told in six words that this person is
mostly guessed, and watched the guess appear on his skin.

**3.000-10.000 s.**
- **3.635 s ("AGI").** The arc peaks at about 55°. Most of the head is now paper. Two hairline callouts draw on: `MEASURED`
  pointing to the eye, `INFERRED` pointing to the ear.
- **4.77 s ("in your eyes").** The camera swings back to frontal and pushes in.
- **5.23 s ("eyes").** An extreme close-up of one eye, the most measured part of him. The catchlight turns orange for three
  frames: the spark.
- **5.90 s.** Cut to the wide shot of the Measured Room: he sits on its one chair facing us, the door behind him, its seam
  pulsing with each sung syllable. "your circuits make me nervous," reveals in lowercase italic on the paper wall.
- **7.52 s.** "that's no surprise" starts typing. It finishes at 8.44, 200 ms before the singer reaches "surprise" at 8.64. The
  machine predicted the lyric. Nothing else moves.

**Why a muted viewer stays.**
1. A human face and giant words arrive within the first 2 s.
2. The face turns into paper at 2.85 s, an image no one has seen before.
3. The premise is stated in six readable words, with no explanation.
4. A light behind a door breathes in time with something we cannot hear, which is a mystery.
5. At 8.4 s the text finishes the sentence before the singer, and the viewer sees the joke.

## 3. The protagonist

**The inner arc: from measured to free.**

| Span | State | What we see |
|---|---|---|
| 0-38 s | Measured | He sits and is looked at. He serves (lays a table for one; the empty chair takes the head of it), prays upward (the name "ChatGPT" as a prayer), and works the Chinese room without being able to answer it. He is an object, shown frontally, as testimony. |
| 38-89 s | Walking the house | Agency as motion. The runway walk down the enfilade accelerates and his atoms rearrange. He is exhibited in a vitrine, toasts a vault that protects nothing, loops through the same door three times, and turns a corner into himself ("there you are"). |
| 89-109 s | Stillness | The world freezes into photographs. He pleads ("Gato, please don't let me go") and blinks once, the only living motion in 20 seconds. The room fills with paperclips around him. He sits with his head in his hands while the words orbit him. |
| 109-138 s | Disobedience | He leaves the axis of the photograph and becomes paper: the price of freedom is becoming a guess. The house multiplies into a plan. He stands before the unopened door with his unmeasured back to us. |
| 138-157 s | Coda | "Was it all for show?" The camera pulls back until he is the vanishing point. Then the back wall, and one breath. |

**How the "reconstructed from one photograph" fact is used.** Openly, as the material system of the film, never as dialogue.
Only two things state it in words: the opening HUD line and the title block (`SOURCE: 1 PHOTOGRAPH`). Everything else is
legible from the seam.

The avatar renders show why this is honest rather than a gimmick. On the reconstructed bust the profile has a visible join where
the photographed front meets the guessed back. That is Solaris's dress with no opening, found in our own asset. We turn it into
the film's grammar instead of hiding it.

**The material law (binding for sets, camera and sources).**
- **Measured register (photographic).** Any surface that faces the lens within about 30° of the photograph's axis looks
  photographic. His face, shown frontally, comes from the photo-projected bust or a generated plate. Each room's front wall is
  finished plaster.
- **Inferred register (paper).** Everything the photograph could not see: the sides and back of his head, his back, profiles
  beyond about 45°, every oblique wall. It is rendered as matte paper in the manner of Thomas Demand's life-size paper
  reconstructions of rooms built from press photographs: clean, uniform, no text, slightly too perfect.
- **The seam** between the two registers is shown only where it means something (shots 1, 10, 13, 40, 47, 49, 52).
- **The halftone screen** follows the same law: fine on measured pixels, coarse on inferred ones.

**Face-angle rules.**
- Photographic face within ±30° yaw and ±20° pitch of the lens; extreme close-ups within ±20°.
- 30-60° only in the three deliberate reconstruction shots where the seam is the subject (1, 10, 47).
- Beyond 60° and from behind: the paper figure (3D), or a generated face-free plate with the back in shadow or silhouette.
- Full-body wides are frontal or from behind. Profiles appear only as silhouettes.

**What we never see.**
- His mouth singing.
- Any other person or face. The house has one occupant.
- What is behind the door.
- The machine.
- His profile or back as flesh.
- A screen of any kind.

**What we see exactly once.**
- His blink (29).
- The moment he leaves the axis (40).
- The back of his head filling the frame (52).
- The frame opening to full 16:9, at 140.24 s (it stays open to the end).

**How the 3D body is used.**
1. **The bust.** The photo-projected bust is exactly true at the photograph's own camera, so at 0° our 3D face is the
   photograph. That makes it final-quality for frontal close-ups (1, 51) and for the seam reveals (1, 10, 47).
2. **The paper figure.** A decimated, flat-shaded version of the MakeHuman avatar in paper material. It is the final image for
   every "inferred" beat: all chorus framings, the Ilya door, the back wall, the disobedience turn. Because it is paper by
   design, the avatar's CG look never has to pass as a photograph.
3. **The previs actor.** In the posed avatar every generated shot has an exact 3D version, which also serves as its fallback.
   CMU clips used:
   - slow walk and the retimed runway walk;
   - stop and look up;
   - stop and turn;
   - sit (seated idle);
   - face in hands;
   - lie down;
   - reach;
   - stand and breathe.

   Unused: run, fall back and dance. Their energy belongs to the song, not to him, and the counterpoint is the point.

## 4. Style

### 4.1 Look: Photograph and Paper
A refinement of two of the three looks under test.
- **Measured register.** Filmic monochrome chiaroscuro with the anabology fashion-film polish: a cold, desaturated grade, wet
  black floors, strong backlight through haze, and one orange.
- **Inferred register.** Demand's paper.
- **Point-cloud reconstruction.** Kept to two moments (the atoms in 13 and the hand of points in 28), so it stays a meaning and
  never becomes texture.
- **Deliberately absent.** Particle swirls, glowing brains, circuit imagery, holograms and Pixar-like faces. The HUD is an
  architect's drawing sheet, not sci-fi.

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| INK | `#0B0B0A` | true black for cards, the black void, letterbox bars |
| NIGHT | `#141413` | night rooms, the plan at night |
| COLD SHADOW | `#1C2328` | shadow tint of the measured register at night |
| PLASTER | `#D8D4CA` | finished (measured) walls under the photograph light |

Saturation of the image stays at or below about 0.15 except for VOICE.

### 4.3 Light
Every light has a source, and each source means something.
- **The photograph light.** Soft, flat, frontal, from just behind the lens, neutral white. It is the measuring light, and it
  lights the face in every frontal shot.
- **The raking light.** Low, from the side. It reveals paper and folds.
- **The voice light.** VOICE orange, about 2,200 K. It only ever comes through gaps: under and around doors, through the slot,
  and round the vault door's edge. Its intensity follows the vocal envelope, per frame, from the Demucs vocal stem.
- **The future.** Cold white backlight through the far doorway of the enfilade, in haze.
- **The brightness arc:**
  1. pale paper day (0-16.6 s);
  2. night falls on the first kick (16.6 s);
  3. the chorus room is one stop brighter at each chorus (REV A-D);
  4. grey stills (89-109 s);
  5. the dark door (132-138 s);
  6. black card;
  7. night to white in the climax;
  8. white, then black.

### 4.4 Frame
- **Master.** 1920x1080 at 24 fps.
- **The house (0-138.42 s): 2.39:1.** The image is 1920x804 with 138 px bars. The bars are the margins of the drawing sheet:
  subtitles and the HUD live in them, and the image stays clean.
- **Cards on true black** (6, 16, 50) are full frame.
- **At the final drop (140.236 s) the frame is full 16:9 for the first time, and stays that way.** The proscenium is removed on
  "show". This is one irreversible opening, not odyssey's alternating 2.39/IMAX hard cuts.

### 4.5 Texture, frame rate, boil
- **Halftone as sampling resolution.** A square dot screen: 3 px pitch on measured pixels, 7 px on inferred pixels.
  - For 3D shots it is driven per pixel by a material ID buffer.
  - For generated plates it follows a segmentation mask: face and skin 3 px, everything else 5 px.
  - Anabology's screen is a uniform 4.2 px. Ours changes pitch where it means something: the loop epochs in 23a-c go coarse →
    fine → full, and the atoms dissolve in 13.
- **Grain.** 1.5 % monochrome grain on top.
- **Bloom** on VOICE lights only.
- **Timing.** Everything plays at 24 fps with no line boil (nothing here is drawn). Paper-register figure animation holds on
  twos (12 fps poses) while cameras move on ones: the machine's guesses also have lower temporal resolution. Try this in lookdev
  and drop it if it reads as a bug.

## 5. Motifs (they return and transform)

1. **The door and its seam of light (the voice).**
   - Introduction: under the door behind him, lighting on the first sung word (1-2).
   - Transformations: the slot of the Chinese room; the breathing pillow shot (12); the gaze through the slot (17); the edge of
     the freestanding vault (22); every doorway of the enfilade; the tiny door of the maquette (48).
   - Climax of the motif: THE DOOR at full intensity, never opened (49).
   - Release: all the seams streak past in the climax, and the vanishing point becomes one orange dot (51).
   - It never opens. We never see through it.
2. **The seam (measured / inferred).**
   - His cheek (1); the photograph held as a mask over a paper head (10); the atoms (13); the loop epochs sharpening (23); the
     step off the axis (40); the turntable in the partition (47); his back before the door (49); the back wall (52).
   - It starts as a flaw and ends as the only honest image.
3. **The slip and the clip (paper).**
   - The lyric arrives through the slot as paper (7, 8).
   - The slips accumulate on the floor (12, 17).
   - The paperclips that fill the room each clip a slip (31, 35, 37): the paperclip maximiser is literally the stationery of
     the Chinese room.
   - The slips orbit the chair as "the words around him" (33).
   - The paper figure is his inferred body, made of the same material as the machine's thinking.
4. **The axis (the photograph's line of sight).**
   - Introduction: the one-point perspective of every locked-off room.
   - The enfilade and its dashed centre line; the vanishing point where the lights converge into Ω (19).
   - The whip that finds a new axis (25).
   - The step off it (40).
   - The pull-back along it into the vanishing point (51).
   - The house was never around him. It was behind the lens, inside the one who looks.
5. **The chair** (a prop that tracks who is present):
   - he sits (2);
   - the empty head of the table (4);
   - the chorus chair (7);
   - the empty chair in the pillow shot (12);
   - the guard's empty chair (32);
   - the Shinji chair (33);
   - the tilted chair (44).

- The counterpoint holds are placed against the loudest music: the verse-2 one-take, the frozen breakdown, the Ilya hold and the
  single climax shot. Speed lives in the frame; the cut stays still.
- Rooms in frame are the singularity counter: 1 room, then 24, 10^30, 256, 10^12, then infinity at a vanishing point.

### 6.3 The release (140.24-156.65)
The loudest 12.7 s of the song get one shot. It is the only one in the film where the camera moves faster than he does, and its
cuts happen inside the frame: 44 door frames on a schedule solved from the grid. It is anabology's night-to-white arc compressed
into one breathless pull-back, and 2001's Star Gate rebuilt as an enfilade. The singularity is shown as what the word means in
perspective: all lines converging on one point, which is him.

Then the drums stop and we get the reverse of the opening: not the measured face but the unmeasured back, filling the frame, and
alive (one breath). The bass cuts to black. The last words on screen are the title and a credit that is also a confession:
nobody checked.

**One full 1080p pass.**
- 3D frames: about 1,900 × ~1.7 s ≈ 54 min.
- Composited GEN frames: about 1,670 × ~0.7 s ≈ 20 min.
- Extras (slit-scan, points, recursion): about 15 min.
- Total: about 90 CPU-min, or about 45-50 min on 2 workers. A preview pass takes about 15 min. This is cheap enough to do the
  repeated full watch-throughs the brief demands, many times.

**The animatic is the fallback film.** Every GEN shot is first built as a 3D previs with the avatar (and the previs is the input
to Mode B), so the complete film can be watched to the song in this session. When a plate fails, the previs, restyled into the
paper or silhouette register, is already cut in.

**What each Mode is for.**
- Mode C (a generated performer in our 3D room) is used only for locked-off frontal tableaux (2, 44).
- Walks and moving cameras use Mode B, previs to plate (4, 13, 23, 40): H3 Max 3D-to-video keeps the camera, timing and object
  layout of our render, according to its model page read 2026-09-28.
- Everything else is Mode A.

## 8. Sources per shot, plates and cost

**The rule of sources follows the philosophy.**
- The measured front of him is a GEN face plate, or the bust at 0°.
- His inferred back is ours: the paper figure, or a face-free GEN plate where he is only a silhouette.
- The machine's view (plans, recursion, the partition, points) is 3D.
- The machine's voice is TYPE.

Face shots go to Kling / H3 / Veo, face-free shots to Seedance 2.5, as in GENMEDIA.md. Seedance is face-free only: references
for back views are body-only crops with no face, and a plate with any visible face is rejected at review. Durations are within
each model's limits: Kling 3-15 s, H3 3D-to-video up to 15 s with a 5 s minimum billed, Seedance 4-30 s.

### 8.3 If GEN plates fail moderation or likeness
The film's logic already treats every non-frontal view as inference, so each failure degrades toward a register the film uses
on purpose:
- a frontal face plate falls back to the bust at 0°, which is the photograph and needs no model;
- a walk falls back to a backlit silhouette or the paper figure;
- an environment falls back to our 3D room.

If every GEN plate failed, the film would still play end to end as 3D and TYPE: fewer photographic moments, the same
structure. No shot depends on a plate for its timing, because every beat event (the gauge, flashes, paperclip levels, door
passes, type) lives in our layer.

## 9. Risks and fallbacks

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation / fallback |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Likeness and moderation on the identity plates** (10 face-route video plates plus the P18 still) (Kling / H3 / Veo refusals, identity drift between shots) | The measured register, meaning his real face in motion, depends on them. Anabology's replies show that identity drift is what reads as "slop" | Stage 0 probe on day 1; ArcFace gate on every take; route changes before model changes (GENMEDIA stage 6); the bust at 0° as a photo-true fallback for any close-up within about 15°; silhouettes and paper for walks. Keep the face count low (10 video plates) and the frames simple (one person, plain light) |
| **2. The paper register reads as cheap CG or low-poly kitsch** instead of Thomas Demand | Half the film is 3D, and the paper figure carries the chorus framing four times plus the Ilya and back-wall beats | Lookdev three variants (fine facets, soft folds, pure smooth paper) and judge them at phone size. Rules: low raking light, halftone, the figure small in frame and never lit frontally. Fallback: face-free Seedance silhouettes for the chorus figure, and keep paper for the architecture only |
| **3. Photographic plates don't sit in one consistent house** (matte edges, light and perspective mismatch across ~20 plates) | The idea needs one coherent building | Prefer Mode B (previs to plate: the geometry is ours by construction) and Mode A. Mode C only for two locked-off frontal tableaux, on mid-grey plates. One grade, one halftone and one HUD over everything, and haze in the enfilade to hide seams. Every room exists first as our 3D, so any plate can be dropped |
| 4. Too cerebral for a bouncy 132 BPM song | Stillness can read as pretentious | The hook in 3 s; legible deadpan objects (freestanding vault, guard chair, NIL shelf, text predicting the lyric); 13 hard syncs on real musical events; the acceleration measured in rooms per frame; review at phone size. If the partition (46-48) doesn't read small, fall back to one full-frame room per beat |
| 5. Render time of the climax, points and recursion | 4 cores, shared | Quarter-resolution previews; frame cache for the partition; LOD in the climax; the 2D Droste instead of true portals |
| 6. Repeating a device | "Orbit reveals paper" could wear thin | Limited to 3 uses (1, 10, 47). Other seam beats are different operations: points (13), halftone epochs (23), a step off the axis (40), a static back (49, 52) |
| 7. Echoing odyssey | Same photo, same author | No passport-page bookends, no cross-cut timelines, no alternating aspect cuts, no ticking watch, no gold scar, no flipping corridor. The single aspect change is irreversible and tied to "show"; the ending is the back, not the photograph |
| 8. The Shinji reference may not be the one the user meant | Shot 33 | Built as "Shinji in a Chair" with the words orbiting him; ask the user for the exact post (open question from research) |
